I've added a [portfolio](/portfolio/) to Artetecha. Five projects to start
with, each with screenshots, a short account of the work, and a link to the
live site.

When I [brought Artetecha back](/writing/artetecha-is-back/), I wanted the
site to reflect the practice as it is today. Part of that is architecture
and consultancy. Part of it is making things: working out what a website
needs to do, designing it, building it, and looking after it once people
start using it. That side of the work now has a home of its own.

Think of it as the agency side of Artetecha, with design, development, and
operations in the same hands. It's still me. The portfolio makes that part
of the practice easier to see, and gives us something concrete to discuss
when you have a project in mind.

The first five entries span publishing, education, and this site itself:

- [Jewish-Christian Studies](/portfolio/jewish-christian-studies/): a
  dedicated home for a department at King's Evangelical Divinity School,
  bringing programmes, faculty, and articles together.
- [Artetecha](/portfolio/artetecha/): the bilingual site you're reading,
  rebuilt around a simpler publishing workflow while preserving fourteen
  years of links.
- [Per Grazia](/portfolio/per-grazia/): an Italian-language publication
  for biblical study, theology, and reflection, with room for long-form
  reading and an archive to explore.
- [Gratia Gratis](/portfolio/gratia-gratis/): a ministry and publishing
  hub that brings teaching, books, and a journal into one editorial identity.
- [King's Evangelical Divinity School](/portfolio/kings-divinity/): the
  school's digital home, helping prospective students explore programmes
  and find their next step.

For the four publishing and education sites, I handled design and development
and continue to look after operations. All four run WordPress on Upsun,
using my upsun-wp plugin for platform integration. Artetecha itself uses
Astro on Upsun. The technology follows what each project needs; the work
includes both what people see and what keeps it running.

I'll add more projects as the collection grows, including web applications.
For now, these five give a sense of the work I do and the responsibility
I take for it, from the first design decisions through to ongoing care.
